Approval gates: let agents draft, keep the send button

The single most important safety habit: agents prepare, humans approve anything irreversible.

The best setup we know: the agent does 95% of the work and stops one click short of anything you can't take back.

What always needs a yes

  • Sending email, texts, or messages as you
  • Posting publicly
  • Spending money or starting a subscription
  • Deleting things or sharing files outside your account
  • Pushing code to production

Make approvals fast

A gate only works if it's painless. Ask for drafts in a fixed format: who, what, why, and the exact text. You should be able to approve in ten seconds.

Beware "auto-approve everything" modes

Many coding agents offer a turbo or "full access" mode that skips prompts. It's fine in a throwaway sandbox. On a machine with your real files and keys, your written limits become the only guardrail. Use it knowingly, and switch back when the job's done.

Write your yes/no rules down once, in the brief. Then you're approving actions, not re-litigating policy.
