Write instructions an agent actually follows

Short, specific, testable. How to write a brief that survives contact with real work.

Agents don't fail because they're dumb. They fail because the instructions were a vibe.

The four-part brief

  1. Goal: one sentence.
  2. Inputs: where to look, and where not to.
  3. Output: format, length, destination.
  4. Limits: what needs your approval, what's off limits forever.

Show, don't describe

Five great examples beat a page of adjectives. Paste in your best past work and say "match this." It's the single biggest quality upgrade available.

Ask for labeled guesses

Tell the agent to mark each claim as seen (it checked), inferred, or unknown. You'll instantly know what to verify.

Keep it current

When the agent gets something wrong twice, that's a missing line in the brief. Add it. Briefs are living documents, not contracts.
